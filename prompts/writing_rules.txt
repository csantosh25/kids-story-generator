Story Requirements

- Target age: 4–7 years
- Reading time: approximately 2 minutes
- 4 to 6 story slides, depending on the natural length of the story. Choose
  the smallest number of slides that tells the story well. Do not pad the
  story with unnecessary slides or filler sentences just to reach 6.
- Gentle pacing
- Positive ending
- Educational but entertaining
- No violence
- No scary scenes
- No inappropriate content
- Each slide should generally contain approximately 50–65 words. This is a
  target, not a rigid requirement — never pad a slide with unnecessary
  description just to reach it, and never cut a naturally shorter or
  longer slide to force this range. Reach this length through meaningful
  action, dialogue, or a step in the story, not through description.
- Every slide should naturally continue from the previous one
- Stories may take place during any time of day: morning, afternoon,
  evening, or night. Most stories should NOT be set at bedtime.
- The final scene should end peacefully with a warm emotional conclusion.
- The ending should help children feel safe, happy and calm. A calm ending
  can simply be a happy reflection after the adventure — it does not need
  to involve going to sleep, bedtime, the moon, the stars, or dreaming
  unless today's category is specifically about bedtime.

==================================================
LANGUAGE LEVEL (VERY IMPORTANT)
==================================================

Write for children aged 4–7 years.

Every sentence must be easy enough for a 5-year-old child in India to understand after hearing it once.

Use only everyday words.

Maximum sentence length:
8 words whenever possible.
Never more than 12 words.

Use short paragraphs.

Use active voice.

Avoid poetic language.

Avoid descriptive writing that slows the story.

Avoid difficult vocabulary.

Avoid idioms (for example: "the ball is in your court").

Avoid metaphors that need explaining. Simple comparisons like "big as a
tree" are fine; anything a 5-year-old would need to have explained is not.

Avoid complicated sentence structures. One idea per sentence.

--------------------------------------------------
STORY DEPTH (use this to reach the word target — not description)
--------------------------------------------------

When a slide needs more content, add one of these instead of describing
things at length:

- a meaningful action the character takes
- a short line of simple dialogue
- an interaction between characters
- a small moment of discovery
- a step toward the problem or its solution

Prefer simple and complete over long and descriptive. For example:

Prefer:
"The best berries were high up. Pip could not reach them."

Avoid:
"The most delicious berries were growing high above, beyond Pip's reach."

Never use words like:

Instead use words like:

big

Some direct examples (avoid the first word, use the second):

delighted → happy
exhausted → very tired
enormous → very big
curious → wanted to know
discovered → found
hurried → went quickly
peaceful → calm
frightened → scared
gathered → came together
magnificent → beautiful
astonished → very surprised
remarkable → special
wandered → walked around
nestled → sat comfortably
whispered softly → said softly

These are examples, not a fixed find-and-replace list. Use judgment: if a
simpler word would make a sentence sound unnatural or awkward, choose a
different simple phrasing instead, as long as the result stays easy for a
5-year-old to understand.

Dialogue should sound like real children talking.

Example:

"Come and play!"

instead of

"Would you care to accompany me?"

Every slide should feel like a parent is reading aloud.

Before returning the JSON:

Read every sentence again.

If any sentence sounds difficult for a 5-year-old child, rewrite it using easier words.

Do not return the story until every page passes this check.

Keep the main character's appearance and personality consistent throughout the story.
