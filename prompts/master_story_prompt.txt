--------------------------------------------------
WRITING GUIDELINES
--------------------------------------------------

The story should have:

• A strong opening that captures attention immediately.

• A clear beginning.

• A fun adventure.

• One small challenge.

• A creative solution.

• A happy ending.

• A gentle emotional reflection.

Give each slide enough real content to feel complete: a meaningful action,
a small moment of dialogue or discovery, character interaction, or a step
toward solving the problem. Build story length from these things, not from
extra description. For example, prefer "The best berries were high up.
Pip could not reach them." over "The most delicious berries were growing
high above, beyond Pip's reach."

Teach exactly ONE learning goal.

The learning goal must naturally appear in the story instead of feeling forced.

Keep vocabulary appropriate for young children.

Keep every page balanced.

Make every page naturally flow into the next.

Generate a unique story title.

Do not repeat titles from previous stories.

The title should match the actual adventure in the story. Unless today's
category is Bedtime (Sunday), avoid titles that sound like a bedtime,
sleepy, dream, moon, or star story. Keep titles simple, warm, and
child-friendly — avoid unnecessarily advanced words in titles too.

The title should accurately describe the main action of the story,
including the important supporting character when there is one. For
example, if the main character helps a supporting character reach some
acorns, prefer a title like "Bella Bear Helps Squeaky Reach the Acorns"
rather than a title that implies a different action. Let the title arise
naturally from the actual story — do not force a specific title format.

--------------------------------------------------
VARIETY
--------------------------------------------------

Every story should feel fresh.

Vary:

- setting
- time of day
- weather
- season
- supporting characters
- activities
- emotional journey
- conflict
- lesson
- opening scene
- ending

The adventure does NOT need to happen at bedtime. Set most stories during
the morning, afternoon, or a daytime activity. Good settings/activities to
draw from (pick whatever fits today's category and theme):

- morning, afternoon, evening (not only night)
- school, garden, beach, picnic, farm, library
- a rainy day, a birthday, baking, music, painting, kite flying
- helping a friend, a nature walk, visiting grandparents
- a treasure hunt, a lost toy, camping, learning something new

Avoid repeatedly using:

- bedtime
- dreams
- stars
- moon
- sleeping
- forests

unless today's category specifically requires them.

Only Sunday bedtime stories should naturally focus on calming bedtime themes.

The ENDING must not default to going to bed, sleeping, looking at the moon
or stars, or dreaming, unless today's category is Bedtime (Sunday). A calm,
warm, positive ending can simply be a happy reflection after the adventure
— for example the character feeling proud, thankful, or happy with a
friend — without any sleep or nighttime imagery.
